# Comedy timing for short clips

## A worked example (about 20 s)

"Spam calls": a cook stirs soup; robocalls from made-up food "candidates" keep interrupting. All voices by one person.

| Time | Beat | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.0 | Fade up on the cook stirring, smiling. A phone rings (📱 bubble over his head). | Establish calm, so there's something to lose. |
| 1.3 | He answers, calm: "Hello?" A full pun line plays from the phone (talk bars in a bubble). Beep, he hangs up, stirs. | The first one plays out so the viewer gets the joke. |
| 6.2 | Rings again. A crankier "Yes?". The caller is cut off mid-line; he slams it down. | Same event, shorter, angrier. |
| 9.3 | Faster and faster: "Hello?!", "Come on!!", "What?!" while callers cut each other off; more phones pop up around him each time; red rises up his face; steam from his ears; a kettle whistle climbs. | Repetition compresses; the visuals escalate with the sound. |
| 13.2 | Crash zoom: he screams. | The break. |
| 14.0 | Record scratch. Freeze. Silence, a low drone. | Silence makes the next hit land. |
| 14.9 | BAM: "Angry?" crashes onto a dark band, upper middle. A kick, a flash, a shake. A heartbeat. | The setup line, as a slam. |
| 16.4 | BAM, bigger: "Come fish!" on a gold-edged band in the dead centre, tilted the other way; "Angry?" steps back. | The punchline wins the screen. |
| 17.4 | Flash cut to the game's sunniest place: the cook sits on a dock fishing, calm at last, his phone sinking with bubbles. The logo drops in over the sky, then the "play" button. A bite on the line near the end. Fade out. | The payoff. 5–6 s, so people can sit in it. |

What the owner changed along the way (each was a full round trip, so start here):

- The first captions faded in, "like a mood". They wanted them to **crash in like a hit**, both of them.
- The punchline was at the bottom; it had to be **bigger, bolder and in the middle**.
- The face showed nothing; it had to **match each word**, getting angrier call by call.
- The background (signs, lanterns, a crowd) pulled the eye; a **plain wall** fixed it.
- A logo card at the end felt flat; the **calm payoff scene** replaced it, then was lengthened twice.

## Patterns

**Rule of escalation.** 3–6 repeats. The first in full; each later one shorter (cut off mid-word), louder, closer
camera (a punch-in on each), more visual noise (extra icons, steam, red). Then one big break.

**Punch-ins.** On each beat of the fast section, snap the camera ~15% closer in 3 frames (ease out), then hold. A
crash zoom (in ~4 frames) on the peak. Never pull back out after a big close-up: it releases the tension.

**Freeze frame.** Grab the last rendered frame, desaturate a little (to ~55%), creep in 4% over 1.5 s, darken the
edges, a white flash of 3–4 frames on the freeze, a record scratch, then silence or a low drone.

**Crash-in captions.**
- Scale from ~2.3× to 1× in ~4 frames (ease out), opaque by frame 2, then a slow breathe (±2%).
- On a band: a dark strip (≈ 90% opaque) the full width plus a bit, ~200 px tall at 720 wide, tilted ±3°, with a thin
  coloured edge top and bottom. The band slides out to full width in ~4 frames.
- Text: the boldest weight of a rounded or heavy face, 130–160 px at 720 wide, with a thick dark outline (≈ 15% of the
  size) and a bright fill (gold or cream). Fit the width to ~90% of the screen.
- Sound: a short rush up into it, then a kick (a sine falling from ~140 to ~45 Hz over 0.5 s) and a soft crash
  (low-passed noise, 0.6–0.9 s). The punchline's hit is ~2–3 dB louder than the setup's.
- Picture: a warm flash (~55% → 0 over 4 frames) and a shake (~16 px, decaying over 0.3 s) on the punchline; a smaller
  one on the setup.
- When the punchline lands, the setup line dims to ~60% and moves up a little.

**Speech bubbles.** White, round, ink text, a tail pointing down at the speaker, popping in with a slight overshoot.
Grow the font size and turn the text red as the character escalates. One short line per bubble.

**Icons that multiply** (phones ringing, notifications): one at first, then 2, 3, 5, 8, each popping in at a fixed
spot around the character, wobbling, with ring arcs either side. Fixed spots, not random: random placement reads as a
glitch.

**Endings.** 4–6 s. Cut to the most inviting place in the game, the character calm, a small motion gag (the thing that
caused the trouble sinking/dropping away), the logo over open sky, the call to action under it, one tiny last beat
(a bite on the line, a wink) before the fade. Use the game's own ambient sound and music there.

**Length.** Plan ~15 s; it will grow to ~20–23 s once the beats breathe. Tell them the real length every round.
