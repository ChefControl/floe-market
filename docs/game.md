# Floe Market: how the game works

The full guide to the game for developers and agents: every stage, the economy, saving, the cloud, reports, development, feature flags, tests and the source layout. The [README](../README.md) is the short version for players.

A cozy 3D idle/tycoon game for the browser, in three stages. Stage 1 is a fish market: catch fish from the ice, sell it at a walk-up counter and upgrade the market. Stage 2 rebuilds it as Floe Sushi, a conveyor-belt sushi restaurant with rice terraces on the hillside, which you grow until the whole chain runs itself. Stage 3 goes underground: Mr. Fugu, a shady pufferfish in a trench coat, cuts you into his illegal sumo ring in the cellar under the dock. It's fight night: you cook chanko for the fans, and each boss who walks in with his crowd is beaten by turning his whole crowd with bowls before the bell, until you can buy back all of Mr. Fugu's chips.

## Play

Play it at https://chefcontrol.github.io/floe-market/, or run it locally:

```bash
npm install
npm run dev
```

- **Loading:** a loading screen shows the game's scripts coming in, megabytes so far out of the total (about 1.3 MB, or 380 kB over the wire compressed: there are no image files to fetch, and the only sound files, recordings of the ring's crowd and the casino yacht's room tone, come later, when they're first needed), then stays up while the world is built, until the first frame is drawn
- **Move:** drag anywhere (touch or mouse) for a virtual joystick, or use WASD / arrow keys. The first time on a device, a small card below the player shows how, how to buy, and how to throw things away at a bin (a finger on the joystick on phones and tablets, the keys on a computer, going by the browser's user agent); once you've walked a little it shrinks away into the ⚙️ settings gear, where **Controls** brings it back
- **The pier:** out from the dock's water edge, east of the fish pile, a pier of the dock's own boards runs north over the water and turns west along it, an upside-down L, a step up from the dock with its first boards a ramp. Fish dip a little deeper as they swim under it (and under the voting boat). It's there in every stage; on election day the voting boat moors along its far side
- **Bins:** beside the places things are picked up, to throw away what you're carrying: steel kitchen bins behind the fish pile on the dock, by the water at the pier's foot, by the farmer's rice stack and the rice patch on the farm path (stage 2), and, down in the cellar (stage 3), open oil drums in the hearth room and by the fish circle in the prep hall. Stand at one and press **E** (on a touch screen, tap **Toss**). What's thrown away doesn't come straight back: after a toss, nothing's picked up (the fish pile, the rice, the cellar's plates, machines and counter) till you've stepped out of the spot and back in
- **Tutorial (stage 1):** a new player is shown the market one idea at a time, each when it first matters. A gold arrow bobs over where to go, with a gold ring on the ground under it and a bubble saying what to do there; while that spot is off-screen, the bubble waits at the edge of the screen with a gold tail pointing the way. First the loop, in order: stand on the 🎣 pad to fish, pick up the slices from the pile, drop them on the counter's 🐟 pad, and collect the cash. Then buying, cheapest first: Bigger arms ($30), a level at the 📈 Upgrade square ($40), and the auto harpoon ($90). Each is a goal: a message says what's next ("Next goal: Bigger arms, $30"), and a chip under the HUD shows it with the cash so far and a bar filling up, turning gold once there's enough. Then the arrow leads to it: on a tile, "stand on it and hold E" (or Buy on a touch screen, going by whether the Buy button is in use); on the Upgrade square, which is learnt by buying a level there, "tap" or "click one in the list". Anything bought ahead of its turn counts. When a tile that needs a better rating comes on offer, a tip says what it needs, with the ★ in the HUD pulsing, until it's been in view for 9 seconds. Each idea is learnt by doing it, and the arrow steps aside while you stand where it points (except where you buy, where what to do needs saying). The last of the basics (usually the harpoon) brings up a banner with confetti for 4 seconds: Well done, Tutorial complete, and under it "The rest is up to you. Go make it big!" The rating tip is for later and doesn't bring it back. A player never sees an idea twice: what they've learnt is kept on the device, so Restart doesn't bring it back, and in the save, so it follows a signed-in player to their other devices. Saves from before the tutorial count what they've already done: anything bought means the loop, buying and the harpoon are known (a save since keeps its own lessons, so reloading mid-tutorial skips nothing)
- **Settings (⚙️, top right),** in four groups. *You:* sign in for cloud saves, and **Your look** (below). *Graphics & sound:* **Graphics** (below), which opens into Auto, 🥔 Potato, Low or High and shows the choice on its row, and **Sound**, which opens into the effects, the ambience and the music, each with a mute button and a volume from 1 to 10 (7 is the game's mix; each step is about 2 dB), remembered on the device. *Game:* **Controls**, which opens into How to play (the controls card) and a switch for the Buy button on screen (on unless turned off, kept on the device), and Restart. *About:* **Report a bug or idea** (see [Reports](#reports)); **Buy me a coffee**, which opens the game's [Ko-fi page](https://ko-fi.com/chefcontrol) in a pop-up window of its own (a new tab where pop-ups are blocked), so the game stays put; **Privacy notice**, which opens [the privacy notice](#privacy-notice) in a tab of its own, in every build; and last, **Credits & Copyrights**, which opens into the notices for the music and sound. Like Sound, Controls, Graphics and the credits each open and close with a click. While the menu is closed, a dot on the gear shows the cloud's state. After 10 minutes of play on a device, a toast says once where Buy me a coffee is and the gear pulses, unless the player has already found it
- **Your look (👕 in the settings):** dress the player from the crowd's wardrobe, free: a man's or a woman's build, six skin tones, eight hair colours and cuts (short, a quiff, curls, bald on top, long, a ponytail, a bun or a bob), a cartoon face the way PEAK does them (eyes: dots, round, sparkly, happy ^^, sleepy, lashes, angry brows or a wink; a mouth: none, a smile, a grin, the tongue out, an "o", flat, a smirk or a frown), a moustache, beard, goatee or earrings, glasses, rosy or freckled cheeks, and the colours of your top, trousers, skirt (worn in spring and summer) and shoes (snow boots in winter). Anyone can wear anything; 🎲 mixes up a whole look the way the crowd's are. While the panel is open the camera comes down in close for a head-to-toe look (rising higher if a wall or a counter is in the way), the player turns to face it and tries the look on in spring clothes with nothing on their head, so the hood or a hat doesn't hide it; walking off or Done puts the season's clothes back on over it. The look goes with the save (and to the cloud, for players who sign in), and stays on the device through a Restart
- **Fish:** stand on the 🎣 pad to hook fish; they get chopped into fish slices automatically
- **Sell fish (stage 1):** pick slices up from the pile, drop them on the counter's 🐟 pad, and walk over the cash to collect it. Walk-in customers pay $4 a slice; once the drive-up window is built, drivers on the road buy 4–8 at a time at $6
- **Upgrade:** stand on a price tile (the squares) and hold **E**, or the **Buy** button that comes up at the side, to pay into it. On a computer the button can be turned off under Controls in the settings; on a touch screen, with no E, it's always there. Stand on one for 2 seconds without buying and a reminder says how. Squares are for buying (the tiles, and the Upgrade squares' menus) and circles for doing something (fishing, dropping things off, the casino's games). The tiles lie square with the world, with their icon and price turned to face the camera. Some tiles also need a star rating (see below). The chip under the rating shows the stage and how many of its upgrades are built
- **Graphics (✨ in the settings): Auto, 🥔 Potato, Low or High.** High draws the whole world the way the people are drawn: low poly but rounded, with the small details (the rounded, dressed-up people under Seasons, below; real deck boards, lashed log fences and pilings, fish stalls with crushed ice, a tiled restaurant with a torii and stone lanterns, dry-stone terraces, her house with siding and shutters, and every machine, sled and campaign in the same style). The sea rolls in low-poly facets that glint, foam laps the shore and rings the floes and posts, the paddies and the hot spring ripple, cloud shadows drift over the ground, shadows have soft edges, and roofs, posts and fences get snow in winter, the deck petals in spring and leaves in autumn. Little effects mark the moments that matter: a splash when a fish is pulled out, chips off the board as the cleaver comes down, coins hopping up when cash lands in your pocket and a glint where a customer pays, gold sparkles round a tile when an upgrade is bought, smoke from her chimney and steam from the rice cookers. Low draws everything as it was before all this: block-limbed people, a flat sea, plain boxes and logs, no effects, and the renderer is lighter (a shadow map half the size with hard edges, and no more than one and a half pixels a point). Potato, for very low-end phones, draws exactly what Low draws for less: no shadows, one pixel a point, no smoothing of edges, at most 30 frames a second, two lamps in the cellar instead of four, and High isn't built at all, which saves memory; picking it, or leaving it, shows a Reload to apply button. Either way, whatever stands still is drawn merged with its neighbours, so the scenery takes a few draw calls instead of one for every post and plank, and the icons on the floor tiles, pads, signs and menu tags are drawn in the game's own style rather than taken from the phone's emoji, so they look the same everywhere. Auto, the default, lets the game pick, and shows under the choices which one it's using: it starts on High and watches the frames in two-second looks, and if four of the last five ran under 50 a second or stuttered (not counting the first few seconds, or a pause while the tab is hidden), it drops to Low, and starts there on the device from then on. On Low it keeps watching, and only if the phone still struggles (four looks of five under 25 a second, or stuttering badly) does it drop to Potato, with a toast that says so. If the browser takes the 3D view away while you play (a phone short of memory), Auto drops a step for good (High to Low, or Low to Potato), and a dialog says the 3D view stopped, your game is saved, with a Reload button; if the 3D view can't start at all, it says to restart the browser. Choosing Low or High stops that; choosing Auto again starts over on High. The choice is kept on the device. On Low, the look panel leaves out cheeks and shoes, which Low doesn't draw
- **Upgrade square:** each stage has a 📈 Upgrade square (by the counter in stage 1, by the kitchen line in stage 2, and in stage 3 a 🔨 Craft square at the crafting bench in the cellar's workshop, which sells every room's upgrades too). Stand on it to open the stage's repeatable upgrades and tap one to buy its next level: a better product (prices), marketing (customers) or the crew (speed). The list in the top left shows every modifier in play as a percentage, with your rating counted into Customers (it brings them in ×0.6 at ★1 to ×1.4 at ★5); tap the stage chip to fold it away or back (on phones it starts folded, so the HUD leaves the game in view). Every level shows in the world too (see [Economy](#economy))
- **Fertilizer shed (stage 2):** once the takeout kiosk opens, a little thatched storehouse like the farmhouse goes up on a plank deck off the south end of the farm path, by the water wheel, with a green noren over its door and a lantern that glows at dusk. Stand on its 🌿 square to buy rice fertilizer: compost, then fish meal, then spring minerals, each making the terraces ripen half as fast again ($20,000, $40,000 and $80,000; all three make rice grow 3.4 times as fast). A labelled sack of each one bought lies on a pallet beside the shed, and the HUD's list shows it as Rice growth
- **Stage up:** with all nine market upgrades built, a gold 🏯 tile appears in the middle of the dock: Open Floe Sushi, $12,000. Paying it off plays the stage-up: the counters and the south fence come down, the restaurant, its garden and the terraces go up, and day turns to dusk. Cash, rating and upgrades carry over; the counters' leftover fish and cash move to the restaurant, and Korki's statue moves to the garden (the casino boat stays where it is)
- **Rice (stage 2):** it grows on the terraces west of the restaurant, out through the farm door. The restaurant opens with a small patch of six clumps by the door, which ripens (green to gold) in 15 seconds: just enough for the first customers at a five-star rating, and no faster with upgrades, so more customers need the rest of the terrace. Each planted terrace grows a set amount, ripening in 30 seconds, a little less than the next step of the restaurant (more seats, another chef) eats; upgrades don't speed it up, so planting more terraces is how the restaurant grows, until all three are planted and the fertilizer shed opens. Wade through ripe rice to harvest it, or take bags off the farmer's stack on the path. Once there's a farmer, the runners carry the stack in too, whenever the kitchen line is shorter of rice than of fish, so from then on your hands are free of rice. Fish the kitchen has no room for go back to the pile as you harvest, to make room in your arms
- **Make sushi:** drop fish slices on the 🐟 pad and rice on the 🍚 pad at the kitchen line facing the dock. Your arms carry both at once. The cooks there toss them to a chef inside the bar, who makes a plate (a bag of rice makes two) and puts it on the belt circling them. Once the takeout kiosk is open, the chefs also pack boxes for it, keeping a few ready When the chefs stand idle for want of fish or rice, the cook at that pad holds up a bubble asking for it (🐟? or 🍚?)
- **Sell sushi:** diners in their evening best come up the garden path and through the red gate, sit at the bar, take plates as they pass, and pay $300 a plate at the register by the gate when they leave. Once there are garden tables, some sit out under the red parasols instead: the chefs put their plates on a serving counter by the gate, and a waiter carries them over on a tray, up to four at a time. Waiters never take plates off the belt, and they're only for show: a garden diner stops waiting once their plate is made. Drivers buy 3–6 boxes at a time at the kiosk on the road ($350 a box); their cash lands just inside the east wall
- **Mr. Fugu (end of stage 2):** there's always been a shut hatch in the dock, out by the water; once Floe Sushi is fully built (every stage 2 upgrade bought), someone starts knocking under it, with a ❗ over it and an arrow pointing the way. Walk up to it and the lid lifts a crack: two eyes in the dark under it, and a whisper ("Psst. Chef. Want real money? Come back tonight."). **Wait for nightfall** and night falls over the dock, with a moon over the sea, stars and the lighthouse turning; then the hatch is shoved twice from below, bursts open in a flash and a wall of smoke, and he rises out of the light on his lift, a black shape till the light from above finds him, and the camera comes round over the chef's shoulder as he looks them in the eye, the moon over his. While he's on, the world holds still: nobody hauls, fishes, drives or eats till he's gone. (**Not now** leaves the hatch knocking: walk off and come back to it.) Then he makes his offer, and makes it clear it isn't legal. Answer with a tap: **Deal me in** puts a gold 🐡 tile by the hatch (Buy into the ring, $1,000,000), and **Not today** leaves the hatch on the dock (walk off, then stand on it to hear him out again, that night again: he comes out once a visit), and day comes back. While he's on, the rest of the game goes quiet: only his voice, his bass and his effects play
- **Fight night (stage 3):** paying the buy-in takes you down the hatch to the cellar under the dock: rooms side by side, with thin walls between them, dug into the earth. Every wall round a room or between two is the same waist-high wall under a timber rail, so you see into every room over the ones in front, and the earth outside comes up level with the rails. As in the dungeons of a game seen from above, the rooms are the only lit places down there, and none of their light reaches the rock outside: each room is rimmed by a kerb of rough-cut stone just past its walls' rails, so where it stops reads at a glance, and past that there's only rough broken rock with boulders lying on it, just made out by the wall and gone into the dark a few metres out (the grow room's place too, while it's a secret). In front of the rooms, toward the camera, the rock rises in a broken slope with dark crags on it, framing the bottom of the screen, though never high enough to hide anything in a room, so there's no edge to the world, only the lit rooms in it. Only along the very back do the walls go up high, all to one height under a timber cap: the dock's piles behind the arena (north and west), Mr. Fugu's own wall behind his office and, once it's found, the grow room's back, with the earth banked up behind them to their tops. Every doorway's frame is as high as the next, the fans' tunnels' too. You land at the foot of the ladder, on the landing. The arena is to the west (the ring, a back-alley pit: rice bales stacked two high and roped into a square, packed dirt on top with the ring marked by an old ship's hawser, 河豚 spray-painted in a corner beside a chalk puffer and tallies, pallets for steps east and west, a green dish lamp hung over the middle, a chain cordon on steel posts round its foot and a NO REFUNDS sign; the stands, the soup bar by the hearth room's door, a wall of straw-wrapped sake barrels beside it, the gong and the cashier's window), Mr. Fugu's office is to the north, the hearth room (the big pot, its ledge, and the bowl counter in the corner south of the prep hall's door, out of everyone's way, its circle a small one right against it, so only walking up to the counter picks bowls up) to the south, and through a doorway at the west end of its south wall the workshop (a daiku's heavy low crafting bench, where everything down here is bought, its 🔨 Craft square straight ahead through the doorway, with a plane, a pull saw, chisels in a tray, a mallet, an ink pot and a whetstone on it, and over it a rack lashed with rope, hung with a long saw, a square, a hammer, a coil of rope and a straw hat, and a 火の用心 notice; a tansu chest with black iron fittings, fish crates branded 魚 under the sign, straw-wrapped sake barrels, timber with a net hung to dry over it, a beam being cut on two trestles in its shavings, coils of rope, a heap of net with its glass floats, a bucket for the whetstones, and a short noren and a red lantern at the door) and the prep hall (two dumbwaiters down from the restaurant's kitchen, one for rice and one for fish slices, in a small lift room of their own behind the east wall where nobody walks, each tipping its trays at a hatch onto a wooden chute, planks between bamboo rails lashed with straw rope, down which they slide into the store by the wall (a banded cedar tub for the rice, a slatted crate on crushed ice and salt for the fish, each with a board brushed with 米 or 魚), which keeps a hinoki tray at its foot full; you take from it standing at its circle on the hall's side, peeking out under the wall, so only walking right up to the wall picks anything up; the drying racks along the back and the salt barrels along the front; and across the back, a stone wall with a padlocked iron gate). Every place you work has a marking on the floor, and the walls stop you at their face, a body's width out. Down there, nothing up in the world is heard (only the ring's crowd comes up through the floor) or drawn (it's all still running, just not drawn while you can't see it), and the seasons turn without news. Stand on the open hatch, or at the foot of the ladder, to climb up or down. The restaurant upstairs carries on by itself (see the front, below). Money down here is **Fugu Chips** (🐡, $1,000 a chip, the same wallet), and the HUD's 💪 is how many fans you fed in this fight's preparation, a local's or a boss's (it starts again from 0 with the next fight)
  - **The kitchen:** take rice and fish from the trays over the east wall, standing at their circles against it (only as much as the racks or barrels have room for), put them in a drying rack or a salt barrel (six in each, ten once upgraded; ready after 8 seconds; the racks and the barrels each have one spot to stand on, in front of the first while there's one and moving along to the middle of the row as more are built, and from it every one of them is filled and emptied, the nearest first; with full arms, ready ones swap out one for one for raw ones going in), and carry the ready ones to the pot: four of each (and four mushrooms, once there are mushrooms) make 20 bowls, cooked while you stand there. The pot, the counter and the soup bar each hold their own bowls: a batch goes into the pot (it holds two batches), which ladles them out one at a time, flying onto the bowl counter (which holds 40), its stew going down with each; you carry them from the counter to the soup bar. A flap at the south end of its west counter, by the wall, lifts when you walk right up to it (its top on a hinge, the half-door under it swinging back), so you can step straight through between the arena and the inside of the bar; it shuts behind you, and nobody else uses it. A tag on the pot's front says how many bowls are in it. The fish come down as the market's own blocks of bluefin tuna fillet, lie in the salt, and come out cured brown. Carry the bowls from the counter to the soup bar in the arena: a round counter of old barrel staves in iron hoops with a battered zinc top, a straw rope and a cigarette-burnt indigo cloth under its lip, upturned buckets and a crate for stools, its own pot of chanko steaming in the middle (while there's soup out), and over it a cartwheel of red and paper lanterns hung from a crooked post with the price board nailed to it. You stand at the hatch on its side by the hearth room's door (its flap lifted, a crate of beer inside) and set the bowls out round its top (it holds 30). The pot shows a bubble for what it's waiting on, and the soup bar one when fans are waiting and it's dry
  - **Every fight** goes the same way, about every 47 seconds. **The fans buy soup** (20 seconds): as many as the seats and the arena's fix-up bring come in through the tunnels behind the arena's back walls (the last of them by 12 seconds in) and crowd the floor round the soup bar, and nobody's served before they've walked up to it (about 6 seconds from the tunnel; anyone slower hurries). Each one who gets a bowl steps up to a free spot at the bar for it, a spot of their own all round it (never on the hatch's side: that's yours), pays for it (Mr. Fugu keeps 30%; the rest piles up in straight towers of pink and gold casino chips, a puffer on each, in a 3 by 3 square on the hearth room's floor against its west wall, to be scooped up by walking over them, as cash is up top), turns your orange and goes to sit on the champion's side: on the stands' cushioned benches first, then on the floor round the ring. **They take their seats** (5 seconds): the ones who didn't eat give up and go home, while the wrestlers crouch. **The fight** (15 seconds), which your champion always wins against a local: it's the free show between boss nights. Then the result, and the fans go home. Boss nights are fought differently (the gong, below). The fans are the low life who come down for a fight, yakuza in sharp suits and hooligans in tracksuits, made like the game's people; they walk round the ring, the stands and the soup bar, each at their own pace, look about while they queue, sit down properly, stand up for the charge and cheer in bursts, and shout things in speech bubbles ("Where's the chanko??" at an empty bar). The seasons don't reach the cellar: nobody down there changes clothes
  - **The gong:** stand on its 🔔 pad and its panel puts your champion against the next boss, as a TV sumo broadcast would (their faces, rendered from their own models, in orange and purple either side of VS), with what he brings in chips (👥 his crowd, 🔔 his bell, 🐡 his prize) and your chances in a word on a red seal: Win, Likely win, Maybe, Likely lose or Lose. That's worked out by playing his preparation ahead with you and your crew in it, from where you stand and with what's in your arms, and goes by how many of his fans it turns against his strength, 💪, the fans it takes to beat him (Hakkaku 💪 51 of his 100, Yokobashiri 104, Dobu 399, Samehada 534, Fukure 815: more than half, for the first, and nearer all of them for each stronger one; the gong's card shows it beside his crowd) (120% or more, or all of them, a win, from 100% likely, from 85% maybe, from 60% likely lost). No numbers: you find out for yourself whether you ever had a chance. Ring it whenever you like (**E**, or the button): no fee, no luck. A banner walks him in (入場, his name and crowd, and how many to turn to win). His crowd comes in through the tunnels in his purple, and once they're all round the soup bar (about 6 seconds, while your kitchen keeps cooking) his bell starts, and every bowl on the bar turns one of them to yours (each steps up to a spot at the bar for it, and turns your orange there); then they sit on either side, and the fight is a tug of war between them. A 👁️ Watch button under the strip (or **V**) closes the camera in on the ring from wherever you are, once his fans sit down. A strip along the top of the screen follows it all: the two faces, the fans each way, his bell counting down, a rope in their colours with his goal marked on it, then FIGHT! and the knot pulled one way or the other. Turn as many as his 💪 (the banner says it: 💪 51 TO WIN) and he goes over the rope. How it ends goes by how it went: a win in the first third of the way from his 💪 to all his fans is tight (a reversal at the straw), the middle third a push-out, the top third grand (the champion's big finisher). And every win, whatever the fans gave it, ends in a scene of its own (the slam): the ring held, black bars closing in and the camera in low on the pair at the straw: mash (a tap, a click or Space: a finger or the key shows how, about five a second to get all the way) to drive him back as he pushes back; the push picks the finisher (all the way, the epic; half way, the champion's grand move; short of that, the one the fans gave), which plays with the camera going with him and slows to a crawl and holds just before his big impact, the camera on him and a ring closing on him: flick down (a swipe, a drag, or ↓ or S) to slam him; all the way and a slam on the hold is 6% more of his prize, half way and a slam 3%, and left too long he falls on his own; a loss under half his 💪 is heavy (the boss's big move), within a tenth of it tight, and between a push-out. After, a card stamps the result: 勝, YOU WIN, the prize and one line of what's new; or 負, how many you were short by and the one thing that cost it (the pot waiting on an ingredient, the soup bar standing empty, or a kitchen too slow for his crowd). The five bosses are Hakkaku 🐙 (100 fans), Yokobashiri 🦀 (210), Dobu 🐟 (498, with a 45 s bell), Samehada 🦈 (571, a 45 s bell too) and Fukure 🐡 (1,100, with a minute's bell), and each one beaten opens more of the cellar. Each is a sea creature taken from his name, built on the same jointed body as your champion so he wrestles the whole fight: Hakkaku (八角, "eight corners") an octopus with tentacles for arms and four more under his belt, who squirts ink at the straw and when he lands; Yokobashiri (横走り, "running sideways") a crab with a spiked shell, eyes on stalks and one huge claw, who walks in and off sideways; Dobu (どぶ, "the gutter") a namazu, the mud catfish whose thrashing shakes the earth, with swaying whiskers and a slimy hide, whose stamps shake the ring; Samehada (鮫肌, "shark skin") a scarred shark with a grin of teeth and a tall back fin; and Fukure (膨れ, "swollen"), Mr. Fugu's own champion, a scowling porcupine puffer who blows up into a ball of spines for the glare, the charge and whenever he's winning, and goes down like a balloon when he's beaten
  - **The grow room:** beating the first boss opens the iron gate in the prep hall's low stone back wall, and the wall comes down: behind it, where there was only earth, a grow room with its first mushroom bed, its crop ready to pick (and mushrooms on the ledge for one batch); more beds and a picker to tend them are sold on its square. From then on each batch needs a mushroom too, and mushroom bowls pay double
  - **The Undertow:** from the first night in the cellar, a heavy iron-banded door stands in the arena's south wall, between the ring and the soup bar, in a frame of iron posts and timber back into the earth, a red lantern hung from an iron bracket by it. Walk up to it and you knock: nobody answers. Beating the second boss (Yokobashiri, whose card says there's a way south) changes that. Once his card's away and the next fight's begun, Mr. Fugu wants a word: a "!" over him at his desk, and an arrow at the edge of the screen. Knock at the door before you've seen him, and a slot slides open on a pair of eyes: "Password?" … "Beat it." Step up to his desk and the camera comes in on him while the cellar winds down and holds still (whoever's walking gets where they're going, then nothing moves): "Nice work on the crab, Chef." "Some friends want to meet you." He slides his matchbook across the desk: "Door by the ring. Show 'em this." (a tap for each line, his voice a low blip a letter as in his first meeting, the doorman's lower still). Then the matchbook comes up close to look at, the room dimmed behind it: red with a gold border, THE UNDERTOW and a 🐡 on its front, a striker and "Knock twice. Show this. — F." on its back, pink match heads along its top. Drag it (mouse or touch) or use the arrow keys to turn it about, it drifts on a flick and sways a little when left alone, and Pocket it puts it away. The games play by the rules casinos commonly use, house edge and all:
  - 🎡 **Roulette:** a European wheel with one zero. Bet on red or black (pays ×2; the zero loses them) or green, the zero (pays ×36). Your chips go down on the box printed on the felt; the wheel spins one way and the ball runs round the rim the other, slows, drops in with a few hops and settles in its pocket as the wheel stops. Winnings come out beside your chips and both slide back to you; a loss goes to the croupier. A lit board on a short post beside the wheel shows the last numbers, low enough to stay in view at the table
  - 🃏 **Blackjack:** a six-deck shoe. Hit, stand or double on your first two cards, and split a pair once (any two tens count as a pair) into two hands played in turn, with doubling after the split; split aces get one card each, and 21 after a split isn't blackjack. The dealer stands on every 17 and checks for blackjack first. Blackjack pays 3 to 2. When the dealer shows an ace they offer insurance, half your stake, paying 2 to 1 if they have blackjack, or even money if you have blackjack yourself. The cards are dealt as in a casino: each slides out of the shoe face down and turns over, the dealer's second stays face down, and the totals float over each hand. When the dealer plays they turn that card over slowly, then draw. A double goes down sideways; on a hard 10 or 11 its card stays face down (the total reads "11 + ?") until the dealer is done, then turns over. A split moves the pair apart and matches the chips, and at the next deal the last hand is swept into the tray. A fresh shoe is shuffled in front of you: the dealer gathers the tray and the shoe into one stack, riffles it three times and puts it down for you to cut, sliding a yellow cut card into the stack (at least a deck in from either end). On Cut the cards above it go to the bottom, the dealer sets the cut card a deck from the back, loads the stack into the shoe and burns its top card into the tray, the only card burned. When the cut card comes out it's laid by the shoe, the round finishes, and the dealer shuffles again. Walk off mid-shuffle and the dealer cuts for you; a shuffle that hasn't started waits for you to come back
  - 🎰 **Slot machine:** three reels, one line. Three of a kind pays 7 ×100, 💎 ×50, 🐟 ×25, 🔔 ×15, 🍋 ×10, 🍒 ×8, and two cherries pay ×2: about 97% back. - **Easy on the ear**, following what's known about which sounds people find pleasant:
  - Consonant notes: effects on the major pentatonic, and two-note effects a fifth or a fourth apart (simple frequency ratios).
  - No half steps in the piano's chords, where they'd sound rough and muddy that low.
  - No held clashing notes in the tune (the jazz "avoid note"); they're only allowed as quick passing notes.
  - Harmonic overtones for anything heard a lot. Bells, with their out-of-tune overtones, are kept for one-off news.
  - Every sound turned down by up to half around 3 kHz, where the ear is most sensitive. Coin and pick-up runs go round within their octave instead of climbing into the shrill.
  - Sounds that repeat a lot (footsteps in snow, roulette clicks) kept out of the sharpest band.
  - Only slow wobbles (gentle detune and tremolo), never fast buzzy ones.
  - Unhurried tempos of 82 to 96 BPM.

### Saving

Progress saves automatically on the device, including your reviews, the season, what the tutorial has shown you and everything along the supply chain (the pile, the counters, the kitchen line, plates on the belt, workers' loads, the terraces' stack, and in stage 3 the whole cellar: its machines and what's in them, the ledge, the counter and the soup bar, the crew, the bosses beaten, what you're carrying down there, and whether you were down in the cellar; a boss's rush cut short by closing the page is called off, neither won nor lost): every few seconds, whenever the page is hidden or closed, and after each upgrade. Cash, fish and rice that are mid-air are counted, as are diners' unpaid bills, so closing the tab at any moment loses nothing.

- **One tab at a time.** If the game is opened in a second tab, the older tab stops saving and says so, so it can't overwrite newer progress.
- **Updates don't reset progress.** Saves carry a format version and older saves are migrated on load, keeping cash, rating and upgrades. Saves from before the two stages stay in stage 1, except those that had both the old restaurant west of the dock and every market upgrade: they go straight to stage 2, keeping the restaurant's upgrades, with the old kitchen's fish and the counters' leftovers on the kitchen line and the cash at the register. Everyone else gets back what they spent on the old restaurant, its cash and its unsold plates. A save from before stage 2's money went up tenfold (October 2026) that's in the middle of stage 2 has its cash, and what it's paid toward a tile, multiplied by ten. A save that can't be read is kept under `floe-market-backup` instead of being overwritten.
- **Storage that sticks.** The game asks the browser to keep its storage (`navigator.storage.persist()`). On iPhone, Safari clears website data after 7 days without a visit; adding the game to the Home Screen avoids that (note that the Home Screen app keeps its own separate save).
- **Restart** (in the ⚙️ settings) erases progress after a second, deliberate tap; a quick double-tap is ignored. Signed in, it starts the cloud save over too.

### Cloud saves

**Sign in with Google** (in the ⚙️ settings) and your game follows you to any device or browser, including the iPhone Home Screen app. Playing without signing in works exactly as before.

- **Syncing:** signed in, the game syncs with your account every 30 seconds and whenever the page is hidden. Open it on another device and you carry on where you left off. The page reloads once to load the newer game, and says "Loaded your game from the cloud".
- **Each sync** compares this device and the account with how they were at the last sync. If only one has moved on, it wins.
  - Both have progress the other hasn't seen? This happens the first time you sign in on a device that already has its own game, or after playing on two devices offline. The game asks which one to keep, showing each one's stage, cash, upgrades and when it was last played.
- **Offline:** the button says so, and the game keeps saving on the device until it can reach the cloud again.
- **Signing out** keeps the game on the device.
- **Saves are private:** each one is one Firestore document, `saves/{your account id}`, which only you can read or write (`firestore.rules`).
- **Download:** Firebase is a separate script (about 160 kB compressed). The loading screen fetches it with the game's own; it starts a few seconds after the game does, or straight away on a device that's signed in, so the sign-in window opens the moment you tap.

**Setting it up** (free Spark plan, no billing needed; the sign-in button stays hidden until this is done). Menu names are as of October 2026:

1. In the [Firebase console](https://console.firebase.google.com/), create a project. Google Analytics isn't needed.
2. **Security, Authentication, Sign-in method:** enable Google. It asks for a support email.
3. **Security, Authentication, Settings, Authorized domains:** add `chefcontrol.github.io` (GitHub Pages), and `localhost` to test with `npm run dev`. Projects created since April 2025 don't include `localhost` by default.
4. **Databases & Storage, Firestore:** create a database in the **Standard** edition (the one with the free quota), in production mode, in a location near your players. Then paste `firestore.rules` into its Rules tab and publish.
5. **Project Overview:** add a Web app (the `</>` icon), and copy its `apiKey`, `authDomain`, `projectId` and `appId` into `src/cloud.config.ts`. Firebase's docs say these can go in public code. Keep the API key limited to Firebase's APIs, which is how Firebase creates it; the security rules are what protect the saves.

The free quota (50,000 reads and 20,000 writes a day) covers about 150 hours of play a day. Each player online reads once and writes at most once every 30 seconds.

### Scoreboard

The 🏆 button (top right) lists the 20 players who have held the most cash at once, and the furthest stage each has reached. It shows once cloud saves are set up.

- **Getting on it:** sign in. Your best goes up as your game syncs, and only ever goes up: spending doesn't lower it, and neither does a Restart.
- **Names:** players show by the full name on their Google account, never an email. Your row is picked out; if you're not in the top 20 your own best shows under the list. Not signed in, it shows this game's best and a button to sign in.
- **Data:** one Firestore document per player, `scores/{your account id}`, with that name, the best and the stage. Anyone can read the scoreboard; only you can write your own entry (`firestore.rules`). A project set up before the scoreboard needs `firestore.rules` pasted and published again, or the scoreboard says it can't load; so does one set up before stage 3, whose rules only accept stages 1 and 2 (a stage 3 player's entry would be refused).
- **Quota:** opening the scoreboard reads up to 20 documents. A signed-in player writes their entry at most once per sync, and only when their best or stage changed.

### Wall of shame

On the casino boat, a sign hangs from the wall behind the bar, over its window end: the worst bet ever made at the boat's games, and whose it was. Step on the 💔 pad in front of the bar by it, and **Wall of shame** opens the ten worst bets of all, each with who made it, the amount, what it was on and the date.

- **A bet:** one round at roulette, blackjack or the slots, and what it lost: everything staked on it (blackjack's doubles, splits and insurance too) less whatever it paid back. Each player is on the wall once, with their worst.
- **Getting on it:** sign in. Your worst goes up the moment you make it, and only ever gets worse: a smaller loss, another device or a Restart doesn't replace it. Not signed in, your worst is kept on this device and shows among the rest only to you, with a button to sign in.
- **Data:** one Firestore document per player, `shame/{your account id}`, with your name (as on the scoreboard), the amount, the game, what it was on, whether it left you broke, and when. Anyone can read it; only you can write your own entry, and only with a bigger loss (`firestore.rules`). A project set up before the wall needs `firestore.rules` pasted and published again, or the wall can't load and nobody's bets go up.
- **Quota:** the wall reads up to 10 documents when the player boards the salon, once a minute while they're in it, and each time they open it. A signed-in player writes their entry with each new worst bet, and at most once per sync.
- **Demo build:** it never goes online, so the wall only has the player's own; `&shame` in the address puts up ten made-up bets (`src/fuguDemo.ts`).

### Reports

**Report a bug or idea** (🐞 in the settings, under About) sends a note to the developer. It shows once cloud saves are set up. Signed out, the row says "Sign in to send a report" and signs in. Signed in, it closes the settings and opens a window of its own over the dimmed game. ✕, Cancel, a tap on the game around it or Escape close it, and a note not yet sent is still there next time.

- **The form:**
  - Bug or Idea, and a note of up to 1,000 characters.
  - A picture of the game, taken the moment the window opens.
  - **Draw-a-Bug**, the red crayon button on the picture's corner (**Doodle It**, yellow, for an idea), or a tap on the picture: opens it big over the game to circle what's wrong with a finger or the mouse, in red (yellow for an idea). Undo takes back the last line, Clear all of them, and Done keeps them, ready to change again; Escape puts it away without the new lines. The picture goes with the lines on it.
  - Two tickboxes, both on each time it opens: **Include this picture** and **Include my game state**. The game state is the save (progress, look and settings) plus where the player is standing and facing, what's open on screen, the season, the sound's mutes, the cloud's state and how many minutes they've played this visit.
- **What the player is told:** above Send, **What's sent** says in a line what goes ("your note, this picture and your game state") and changes as the boxes are ticked. Opened, it lists what each of those is, what always goes and what never does (their name and email), and that the picture and game state are deleted after 30 days (each report says how long its player was told, so the intake keeps it that long; reports sent before this said 7 days). Unticking a box adds a warning under it: "Without the picture, it may be hard to see what went wrong", or "Without your game state, the developer may not be able to make it happen again to fix it".
- **Always sent:** the commit the game was built from, the graphics setting and frame rate, screen size, whether it's a touch screen, the browser, and the last 10 errors the game hit this visit.
- **Sending:**
  - A moment over the game: for an idea, a light bulb flickers on bright with a chime, "Bright idea!"; for a bug, a fly swatter comes down on a scurrying bug with a swish and a smack, "Bug squashed!". That's all a player sees when it goes; words come up only when it doesn't (refused, or waiting to go online).
  - A player can send 20 reports an hour, at least a second apart; the hour starts with the first. One sent within a second of the last waits out the second and then goes. A 21st says how many minutes until they can send more, and the window stays open with the note in it.
  - When the cloud can't be reached, the report waits on the device (the newest two at most) and goes when the player is next signed in and synced. The thanks says so.
- **Telling players:** a player who played before reports came gets a toast once, 3 seconds into their game (when nothing else is open): "🐞 You can now report bugs and ideas from the ⚙️ menu". New players aren't told, and nor is anyone who has opened it already.
- **When something breaks:** the red error box has a **Report this** button, which opens the form with the error written in.
- **Data:** each report is a Firestore document in `reports/`, with a name of 20 letters and digits.
  - Only players signed in with Google can add one, and nobody can read, change or delete one from the game.
  - `limits/{your account id}` counts reports: at most 20 in an hour from the first, at least a second apart, and then the count starts again.
  - `config/reports` (`{open: true}`, made by hand in the console) switches reports on and off.
  - All of this is in `firestore.rules`; the rules are tested against the Firestore emulator in `test/rules/`.
- **Becoming issues:** a workflow in the private repo `ChefControl/floe-market-reports` turns each report into an issue every 30 minutes, with the picture inline. It deletes pictures and saves after 30 days (7 for reports sent before the game said 30). Its code and setup are in [`intake/`](intake/README.md).

### Privacy notice

`public/privacy.html` is the privacy notice for the web game and the Steam version, on the site at `/privacy.html` and linked from the settings (Privacy notice, under About). It says what each version keeps, why, where, for how long and who sees it, and how to ask to see or delete it.
- **It's the owner's text.** Any change to what the game keeps or sends (a field in Firestore, what a report carries, how long the intake keeps it, a new service or a request to another site) changes the notice in the same PR, with the owner's OK, and moves its "Last changed" date.
- **It stands alone:** no script, nothing from another site; its heading font is the game's own Baloo 2 from `public/fonts`.
- **Steam:** the store page links it too (#153), and the Steam build keeps the settings row (#160).

## Development

TypeScript + [three.js r186](https://threejs.org/), bundled with [Vite](https://vite.dev/). All textures are drawn at runtime on canvas. The fonts and the emoji ship with the game (`public/fonts`, cut down to what it writes by `scripts/fonts/build.py`), so it fetches none from the web.

**Open-source licences.** The build credits the third-party code it actually bundles (`scripts/licences.ts`): it writes each package's licence text to `licences.txt` beside the game and lists the packages under Credits & Copyrights. It's what's bundled, not `package.json`, so dev tools never show, and a build without Firebase leaves it out. The build stops on a GPL, LGPL or AGPL licence, or one it can't read, until the owner approves that package and licence in `APPROVED` (Steam needs Valve's agreement before a game ships GPL or LGPL code). The Electron build hands in its own notices (Electron, Chromium, ffmpeg) as `licences({ extra })`, checked the same way.

| --- | --- |
| `npm run dev` | Dev server with hot reload |
| `npm run typecheck` | Type-check only |
| `npm run build` | Type-check and build to `dist/` |
| `npm run preview` | Serve the production build |
| `npm test` | Unit tests with coverage (fails below the thresholds in `vite.config.ts`) |

| `npm run test:rules` | `firestore.rules`' tests, in the Firestore emulator (needs Java 21; CI runs them on every pull request) |

`SIM=1 SIM_MINUTES=60 SIM_OUT=sim.txt npx vitest run test/balance.sim.test.ts` has a bot (`test/bot.ts`) play a fresh game for an hour of game time, in a few seconds, and writes when it bought each upgrade and how its money and earnings grew minute by minute. Use it to check the balance after changing prices; `test/softlock.test.ts` also uses the bot to check a whole game never stalls. Add `SIM_STAGE=3` to play the ring from just after the buy-in instead: down in the cellar the bot runs whichever lines have no hire yet, cooks, carries bowls to the soup bar, buys the cellar's tiles, rings the gong once it says the boss's whole crowd will turn, and finds its way round the cellar's furniture.

### Feature flags

`src/flags.ts` can keep stage 3 from players: with it off there's no hatch in the dock and Mr. Fugu never knocks. It's open in the live game, since the deploy builds with `VITE_STAGE3=on` (`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`); setting that to `off` takes it back. It's on in the dev server, the tests and the playtest build (`VITE_FUGU_DEMO=1`). In a build without it, open the game once with `?stage3=on` in the address to switch it on for that device (`?stage3=off` switches it back off). A save that has met Mr. Fugu switches it on wherever it's loaded, so a cloud save carries on on another device, and a player who's been down there keeps stage 3 even if it's taken back.

It also decides whether there's **Buy me a coffee** (Ko-fi): only a game built or served with `VITE_KOFI=on` has the settings row and the 10-minute nudge, as the live game does (the deploy sets it). Without it, the default included, the row and its ko-fi.com link are left out of the page itself (`vite.config.ts`), so the Steam build, which mustn't link to payment pages outside Steam, never sets it. To see the row in the dev server, run `VITE_KOFI=on npm run dev`.

It also dates the voting boat: `ELECTION_DAYS` brings it in on 27 October 2026 by the device's own date, and nothing of it is built on any other day. `?vote=on` in the address brings it in on any date, for that visit only, and `?vote=bye` does too and sails it away a minute after it's moored.

[docs/balance.md](balance.md) sets out the principles the game is balanced by, stage by stage, the numbers that show when something is off, and where each upgrade stands.

[docs/graphics.md](graphics.md) is the guide to drawing anything new: the Graphics setting and its menu row, building High and Low versions, keeping the game's luck intact, staying fast on phones, and checking the result. Agents get it through the `graphics` skill (`.claude/skills/graphics`).

`.github/workflows/deploy.yml` runs the tests on every pull request and push. On `main`, it builds and deploys to GitHub Pages only if they pass.

Each deploy removes the last one's files, and GitHub Pages lets browsers keep the page for 10 minutes. A page kept from before a deploy, or a tab left open across one, would ask for files that are gone: the game wouldn't start, or Firebase wouldn't load. So the page reloads once to fetch the new version (an inline script in `index.html` for the game's script, which the loading screen adds even when its own fetch fails, so it's still seen to fail; `src/errors.ts` for Firebase), at most once a minute in case the site is actually down.

`overrides` in `package.json` lifts Firestore's `@grpc/grpc-js` (pinned to 1.9.x, which has known vulnerabilities) to a patched release. Only Firestore's Node.js build uses it, so the game in the browser is the same either way. Drop the override once `@firebase/firestore` depends on 1.13.6 or later.

### Tests

[Vitest](https://vitest.dev/) in jsdom. `test/setup.ts` stubs the two things jsdom lacks (the WebGL renderer and 2D canvas drawing) and seeds `Math.random`; everything else, three.js included, runs for real. `loadGame()` in `test/helpers.ts` loads a fresh copy of the whole game, optionally from a save, and steps it with `tick()`, so most tests read as "set up a situation, run a few seconds, check the outcome".
